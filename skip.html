Technical Advisor for Wearable Maternal Health Monitoring

Skills: Technical Advising, Onshape, Wearable Product Design, Rapid Prototyping, User Research, Figma

Project Overview

Project Stardust was developed in collaboration with Skip as an at-home wearable concept for maternal health monitoring. I served as a technical advisor to a cross-functional team of engineers and designers, helping translate user needs into the mechanical architecture of the belt, sensor-array packaging, prototyping strategy, and companion-app experience.

My work focused on creating a compact wearable form factor that could integrate multiple sensing modalities while remaining approachable and practical for repeated use at home.

User Research & Product Direction

The team combined literature review and competitive analysis with 84 survey responses and 31 discovery interviews across first-time, second-time, high-risk, rural/remote, and underserved mothers.

Research consistently showed that anxiety was highest between appointments, but that raw data without context could create more stress. Users trusted their providers most, and wanted continuous information to support rather than replace clinical care. Bulky belts and handheld ultrasound products also felt impractical or intimidating, which pushed the concept toward a softer, lower-profile wearable with clear, interpretable feedback.

Belt Architecture & Sensor Array

The wearable architecture used three compact sensor arrays distributed across the belt to collect data from multiple abdominal locations. The arrays were connected through USB-C cabling to a shared battery pack positioned in an elastic pocket, keeping the power system accessible for charging while allowing the sensing modules themselves to remain compact.

Each sensor-array housing was designed around a stacked package containing 1 IMU, 1 ultrasound sensor, 2 EHG electrodes, and 2 microphones. The concept also integrated the FPGA and speaker assembly into the pod stackup. I modeled the housing and wearable layout in Onshape and iterated through rapid prototypes to balance sensor placement, skin contact, cable routing, thickness, and comfort.

Companion App

I also contributed to the UX and functional design of the companion application. The app was structured around wearable onboarding, daily snapshots, ultrasound visualization, trends, and tiered alerts. Rather than presenting sensor streams on their own, the experience was designed to translate measurements into understandable context and make information easier to share with a care team.
